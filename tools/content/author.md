## Scientific biography

For more than forty years, Nona Dmitrievna Dronova's scientific, teaching and expert work has been devoted to gemmology, the technological mineralogy of diamonds and coloured stones, and the scientific examination and appraisal of precious objects. She is the author of more than 79 scientific and methodological works, including eight monographs, and of the encyclopaedia *Jewelry Business* (2009), as well as more than 280 open-access research articles with DOIs on Zenodo. She takes part in professional and international events connected with CIBJO, the WFDB and EGL.

In recent years Professor Dronova has carried the rigorous methods of mineralogy and gemmological optical microscopy over to the authentication of Chinese antiques — porcelain, jade and bronze. A work of art is treated not only as an artistic creation but as a material document of its time, preserving the traces of its manufacture and of centuries of natural ageing. This work has resulted in the monographs *What You Need to Know About Antique Chinese Porcelain* (2016) and *What You Need to Know About Antique Chinese Jade* (2017), and in two digital diagnostic atlases documenting more than 140 reference objects from the Neolithic to the Qing dynasty.

## Research methods

### Porcelain and ceramics

**1. Gemmological optical microscopy (GOM).** A non-destructive study of the macro- and microstructure of the surface, glaze and body with a digital optical microscope. It reveals the technological and post-technological features formed during manufacture and natural ageing. No samples are taken, so the method can be applied to objects of the highest museum value.

**2. The BEINOS Method.** The author's multi-level analytical system for the attribution and authentication of ancient Chinese porcelain ([doi:10.5281/zenodo.16786915](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16786915)). It integrates four domains:

1. **microstructural analysis** — crackle, Pb–Si phase separation, natural iridescence;
2. **chemical–structural analysis** — oxides and colorimetry referenced against museum standards;
3. **artistic–aesthetic evaluation** — proportions, tonal harmony, integrity of the artistic idea;
4. **technological–historical assessment** — forming, firing traces, diagnostics of the base and mark, verification of provenance.

Each object is assessed with a standard protocol using weighted coefficients and data-confidence factors, and is assigned to one of four categories: *museum-grade original — high-certainty original — requires further examination — likely reproduction*.

The microstructural part of the method examines **six parameters**: (1) glaze structure and density; (2) configuration and hierarchy of the crackle; (3) the glaze–body transition zone; (4) micro-inclusions; (5) the thermal history of firing; (6) signs of natural ageing. Its key principle is **multi-parameter concordance with the reference standard**: authenticity is confirmed only by the complete set of features, never by a single one.

**3. The author's optical diagnostic effects** — a series of concepts observable without damaging the object:

- **DEE — Dry Earth Effect (2025).** A dynamic shift of light and shadow within crackle zones when the object is rotated through 180° in diffuse daylight: the internal fissures appear dark from one side and light after rotation. The effect is caused by V-shaped micro-fissures beneath the glaze layer, not by the colour of the glaze. It is a non-destructive, reproducible test for Northern Song Ru ware, applied together with colorimetry, crackle morphology and kiln indicators ([doi:10.5281/zenodo.16724189](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16724189)).
- **SDE — Sapphire Depth Effect.** The visual depth and inner luminosity of the cobalt-blue ground of imperial wares of the Qianlong period.
- **LIE — Lead Immiscibility Effect.** Metastable liquid–liquid phase separation in the PbO–SiO₂ system as a lead glaze cools, visible through the intact glaze as a wavy micro-stratification; characteristic of genuine lead glazes such as the imperial yellow glaze of the Chenghua period.
- **PLIC — Pb-Leaching Iridescence Criterion.** Over centuries, Pb²⁺ ions are leached from the glaze surface, leaving a silica-enriched film 50–500 nm thick that acts as a thin-film interferometer and produces a blue-silver iridescence — direct optical evidence of centuries of natural ageing.

**4. Genetic analysis of authenticity** ([doi:10.5281/zenodo.16755260](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16755260)). The object is treated as the result of the chain "raw material — technology — firing — ageing"; the features of every link must be consistent with one another.

**5. The attribution and quality-examination algorithm** (monograph, 2016): ten stages from determining the country of production to assessing rarity; a critical-analysis table of the object's consistency with its period; categories of complexity of artistic decoration; chronological, socio-cultural and quantitative criteria of rarity.

**6. The scale of attribution certainty** used in the atlases: *Confirmed — Probable — Proposed — Disputed*.

### Jade

**7. Surface Transformation Analysis (STA).** The object of study is not the composition of the stone — ancient raw material is also found in forgeries — but **the surface of the object as an archive of its geochemical history in burial**. Five stages in the formation of the secondary layer are distinguished: surface activation by working; ionic infiltration; primary mineralisation by iron and manganese compounds; crystallisation of hematite, goethite and lepidocrocite; structural stabilisation. The criteria are secondary iron formations on fibre ends, microcracks and traces of ancient working; pseudo-reticulate and linear structures of iron and manganese migration; zones of leaching; irregular micro-relief — and above all the **spatial consistency** of all these features.

**8. Signs of natural ageing in jade** (monograph, 2017): ring-shaped iron-rich formations crystallised on the surface of carved artefacts; the zoning of external corrosion; contamination and staining patterns that indicate a forgery.

### Bronze

**9. Trace Element Tail Analysis (TETA).** Ancient bronze is treated as an **inherited "ore — alloy — patina" system**. What matters diagnostically is not the absolute content of elements but their relationships: stable associations Cu–Sn–Pb, Cu–As–Sb and Fe–Ni–Co; a smooth decline in concentration from inclusions towards the matrix (the "tails"); uneven distribution of lead; consistency between alloy and patina. Modern alloys are homogeneous and lack such gradients.

**10. The study of noble patina.** Patina is regarded as a continuation of the geochemical system of the alloy. The method distinguishes noble from ignoble patina by its layered mineral structure, including the formation of malachite, and detects artificial patination.

**The unifying principle** of all the methods: an ancient object is a system of **"material — environment — time"**. Authenticity is determined not by appearance but by the stable structural and geochemical traces of the material's long interaction with its environment.

## Proposed for the first time

- The **BEINOS Method**: four domains of analysis, weighted coefficients and a four-level expert conclusion.
- The series of optical effects **DEE, SDE, LIE and PLIC**, observable without damaging the object.
- The **STA** method for jade and the **TETA** method for bronze.
- The single principle of **"material — environment — time"** for three materials.
- **Ring-shaped iron-rich formations and the zoning of corrosion** as signs of the antiquity of jade (2017).
- Digital **diagnostic atlases** with a uniform structure for recording every observation.

## Scientific novelty

The novelty lies in the shift **from connoisseurial judgement to reproducible structural verification**: features are observed according to a protocol, documented by micrography and can be checked by another researcher. For the first time, the methods of mineralogy and gemmology have been applied consistently to all three principal materials of Chinese antiques within a single diagnostic philosophy.

## Relevance

Specialists estimate that between 75 and 95 per cent of the "antique" jades on the market are modern reproductions, and forgeries of porcelain and bronze are becoming ever more sophisticated. Non-destructive methods based on features that cannot be reproduced artificially in a short time are in demand by museums, auction houses, experts and collectors.

- **Expert practice:** objective verification of objects and detection of forgeries.
