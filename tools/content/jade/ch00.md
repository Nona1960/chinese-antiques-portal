# Preface

The purpose of this work is to study the particular features of the natural ageing of jade in burials and to describe the diagnostic signs of its antiquity. The main attention is given to analysing the causes of the characteristic ring-shaped iron-rich formations crystallised on the surface of carved artefacts, and to studying the zoning of external corrosion on the surface of antique Chinese jades. The particular features of noble patinas on ancient bronzes are examined separately.

Nephrite is one of the oldest stones known to humankind. Objects made from it are found in excavations dated to the fifth to third millennia BC. Ancient carvings in nephrite are distinguished by unique artistic and decorative qualities and form one of the most sought-after fields in the collecting of decorative and applied art. At auction, lots of ancient jade carvings can fetch several million US dollars.

Because ancient jades command such high prices, forgeries are numerous: copies of ancient objects are carved from nephrite with modern equipment and then artificially aged to give them the appearance of an ancient patina. Since the principal value of a jade lies in its age, a collector can easily get into trouble. Many experts estimate that between 75 and 95 per cent of the "antique" objects on the market today are modern reproductions. Unfortunately, there is no single, objective scientific method that can establish the antiquity of a jade with certainty.

The results of scientific research, combined with careful observation of the artistic merits of a carved jade object, can provide the collector with evidence that an artefact is genuine — or that it is a modern reproduction.

Growing interest in archaic jade carvings has stimulated the study of their altered surfaces, of the composition of corrosion stains and of the conditions in which they form. The results of such research are of great scientific importance for establishing age and detecting forgeries.

Authentication matters so much because of the enormous number of forgeries and imitations. In recent years the methods of forgery have become increasingly sophisticated, which often makes authentication exceptionally difficult and requires the use of scientific methods of diagnosis.

In deciding whether a carved jade object is authentic, the key task is to determine the technology, the time and the place of its production.

The collector's task is to acquire as much knowledge as possible and to be more attentive and more thorough than the forgers. An analytical and systematic approach to the mineralogical features of nephrite makes it possible to place the diagnosis of antiquity on a scientific footing.

When studying antique jade carvings, it is recommended to analyse documents and sources covering the following subjects:

1. stylistic analysis;
2. the particular features, level and skill of the working;
3. the value of the stone extraction and its rarity (known deposits);
4. the quality requirements for the raw material;
5. the geography and mapping of find sites;
6. the particular features of the mineral composition of the nephrite.

I hope that the material presented in this book will help readers to distinguish jade objects aged under "laboratory" conditions over relatively short periods from genuine ancient jade artefacts.
