Jade

Antique Chinese jade

Jade occupies a unique place in Chinese culture, from the ritual objects of the Neolithic to the carvings of the Qing court. For the expert, every jade carries two records: the stone itself — nephrite, its structure and colour — and the history of its working, preserved in tool marks, polish and drilling.

Authentication rests on the study of the surface: the alteration zones, burial patina and natural transformation that form over centuries and that are extremely difficult to reproduce artificially.

The monograph What You Need to Know About Antique Chinese Jade (Moscow, 2017), a handbook in questions and answers, is published in this library in English translation.

Bronze

Ancient Chinese bronze

Ritual bronze vessels and archaistic objects are studied through their form and decoration, casting technology, corrosion layers and patina. Microstructural and chemical evidence — including the trace elements of the alloy and of the corrosion products — makes it possible to distinguish natural long-term change from artificial patination.

Documented reference objects in jade and bronze are published in the companion Atlas.
